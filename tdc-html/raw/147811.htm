Topic 147811

DPS128 / DPS64 power budget

Subtopics

  1. DC Scale DPS128 Power Calculator

The DPS128/DPS64 card features two power domains with a dedicated power budget. Each domain is able to deliver half of the overall power budget. SmarTest limits the overall power usage, so that the power budget of each domain is not exceeded. Any violation will trigger an error message.

Power budget

For a DPS128/DPS64 card, the maximum total power budget of the raw voltage supplies is 426 W. This budget is distributed equally to two power domains. Within the two power domains, the available budget of 213 W is shared by the output stages of 64 channels (DPS128) or 32 channels (DPS64).

Note

The available power budget mentioned above, is available at the output stages of the power domains.

The total power to be assessed for the individual channels therefore includes not only the power at the DUT, but also the internal power dissipation of the output stage, the power loss at the internal resistor for current measurements and the power loss of internal traces and the pogo cable.

We therefore recommend using the DC Scale DPS128 Power Calculator for calculating the total power consumption and the utilization of the power budgets.

For high current channels, the voltage range needs to be adapted accordingly to minimize the consumed power. For more information refer to section Limiting voltage ranges below.

For more information, refer to section "3 Power Budget" of the DC Scale DPS128/64 HC/HV Cookbook in the SmarTest 7 documentation.

Limiting voltage ranges

To not violate the overall power budget of the card, you must set up voltage ranges for channels that operate in the current range up to 1 Ampere.

You can program the voltage range to any value, but all ranges < 2.25 V will be mapped to 2.25 V. From 2.25 V to 7 V there are 256 voltage range steps with a resolution of ~18 mV per step. Based on this resolution, any programmed value will be mapped to the next higher range.

You can limit the voltage range both in DPS mode and in SIG (PMU) mode:
  • In DPS operation mode with vout_frc_rng in an Equation based level setup. For example:
    DPSPINS VDD
vout         = 1.8
vout_frc_rng = 3.5    #max bump voltage
  • In SIG (PMU) mode with the vForceRange function of the SPMU_TASK class. For example:
    SPMU_TASK force;
force.pin("signal1").vForce(1.8 V).vForceRange(1.8 V)
    Note Switching voltage force range requires a disconnect sequence. To keep the test time low, select a maximum voltage value that is valid for as many cases as possible.
