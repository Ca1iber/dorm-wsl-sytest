Topic 90869

Leakage current

Leakage current is unwanted or undesired current.

Leakage currents are generated by high resistance paths between a nearby voltage source and the current measurement trace.

Leakage current is a function of the conductor area, the distance between adjacent conductors, the volumetric, or surface resistivity of the material supporting the conductors, and the voltage between the conductors.

  • The greater the distance or separation of the conductors the lower the leakage current. The closer the distance between conductors, the higher the leakage current.
  • The larger the area of the conductors, the higher the leakage current.
  • The higher the voltage difference between adjacent traces, the higher the leakage current. Avoid supply traces with high voltages parallel to sensitive traces.

These leakage currents can adversely affect the accuracy of a low current measurement. To minimize and reduce leakage currents use high quality board materials, insulators, and use guarding. See also Shielding and guarding.

Also the solder mask selected has a direct affect on leakage current. A solder mask with a low surface resistivity can render a DUT board useless.
