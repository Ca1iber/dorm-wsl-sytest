Topic 123199

DPS Sharing Diagnostic test scopes

Each of the different diagnostics you can choose to have DPS Sharing Diagnostic perform tests specific functions of particular elements of the load board.

Vterm relays

The Vterm relays disconnect shared buses from the termination power supplies. This diagnostic tests the relays and the related cabling, sockets, and connectors.

If voltages of 0.5 V and 1.0 V are not correctly transmitted when the relays are closed, the relays are reported as stuck open. If they transmit these voltages even when they have been switched to open, they are reported as stuck closed.

This test does not use a short device.

VDD/VDDQ relays

These relays respectively connect the VDD or VDDQ tester channels to the VDD DUT pins and the VDD or VDDQ tester channels to the VDDQ DUT pins.

They are tested by determining whether current is flowing to the correct channel (according to the relay setting, when 0.5 V is applied on VDD or VDDQ). If not, the failing relay setting is reported as having a relay or cable defect. The diagnostics also tests whether there is leakage to the wrong channel when a voltage of 1.2 V is applied. If there is, it is reported as leakage for the relay setting in question.

This test uses short devices. All domains can be tested in parallel, but no more than one site per domain at the same time. That is, if there are four sites per domain, four iterations of the test are needed, regardless of how many domains there are (provided that enough short devices are available).

You are prompted by the diagnostic to insert the short device into the required site.

Overcurrent protection

Overcurrent protection is built into load boards to avoid damaging DUTs by unintentionally high currents on VDD or VDDQ. It protects DUTs by disconnecting the VDDQ tester channel if a site draws current more than 1.2 A.

This test blocks the VDD tester channel and connects the VDDQ tester channel to both the VDD and VDDQ DUT pins. It applies 600 mA and 0.5 V on VDDQ and verifies that the current is not blocked by the protection. It then applies 2.1 A and 1.0 V and verifies that the current is reduced to less than 1 mA. If the 600 mA current is blocked, it is reported as oversensitivity. If the 2.1 A current is not blocked, it is reported as low sensitivity or stuck close.

In addition, the overcurrent reset sequence is triggered and 5.0 V and 600 mA is applied. If this current is still blocked, an overcurrent reset failure is reported.

This test uses short devices. All domains can be tested in parallel, but no more than one site per domain at the same time. That is, if there are four sites per domain, four iterations of the test are needed, regardless of how many domains there are (provided that enough short devices are available).

You are prompted by the diagnostic to insert the short device into the required site.

Note that the overcurrent protection test is reserved for future expansion and cannot currently be enabled.

TDQS relays

The TDQS relays can connect the TDQS# pin unshared to a DUT pin of any site.

The diagnostic first tests whether a current is flowing when 0.1 V is applied to the TDQS# tester channel while there is no short device. Put the relay is in its unconnected state. If more than 100 µA current is flowing, leakage is reported. Put the relay is in its connected state. If more than 100 µA current is flowing, leakage is reported.

Next the diagnostic tests whether a current is flowing when 0.1 V is applied to the TDQS# tester channel while using short device. Open all TDQS relays. If more than 100 µA current is flowing, the error is reported as stuck close. Close the relays in the site under test. If less than 100 µA current is flowing, the error is reported as stuck open.

This test uses short devices. All domains can be tested in parallel, but no more than one site per domain at the same time. That is, if there are four sites per domain, four iterations of the test are needed, regardless of how many domains there are (provided that enough short devices are available).

You are prompted by the diagnostic to insert the short device into the required site.
