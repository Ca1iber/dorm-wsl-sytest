FVI16 TMU pogo pins

The FVI16 card provides a high-voltage time measurement unit (TMU) with a high-speed voltage comparator and high-resolution voltage and current comparators per channel. The inputs of the high-speed voltage comparator are available on separate pogo pins. Some special design rules must be respected when connecting the pins of the high-speed voltage comparator on the DUT board.

The TMU inputs require a proper DUT board design for accurate time measurements. The traces from the TMU input pogo pins to the DUT should be routed with a controlled impedance of 50 Ω. Series termination resistors of 50 Ω must be used at the DUT side of the traces.
Note The TMU pogo pins of an FVI16 channel are used only for measurements via the high-speed voltage comparator. Measurements by the high-resolution voltage and current comparators are done via the FVI16 output pogo pins.

TMU_H#

TMU_H# is the high side pogo pin of the high-speed voltage comparator.

The pin can be connected to the high force (HF#) and the high sense (HS#) pin of the related channel. The pin may also be connected to any other DUT as far as the maximum compliance voltage of the TMU_H pin is maintained. For more information, refer to the "High speed comparator inputs (TMU_H / TMU_L)" in FVI16 TMU specifications.

The pin should be connected via a trace with 50 Ω impedance and a series resistor of 50 Ω must be used at the DUT side of the line.

TMU_L#

This pin is used as shield for the TMU_H# pin of the high-speed voltage comparator. The pin is internally connected to the LF# pin which represents the floating ground of a FVI16 channel.
Note The signal at the low sense pin LS# is the ground reference of the TMU high-speed voltage comparator.
For optimized performance, the shield of TMU_H# must be connected to both the pogo pin TMU_L# and the DUT although TMU_L# is already internally connected to LF#. This will optimize the AC return path of the signal. A series resistor of 50 Ω must be inserted.
CAUTION The pogo pins LF# and LS# must additionally be connected if the high-speed voltage comparator of the TMU is used.
CAUTION A series termination resistor of 50 Ω is mandatory if TMU_L# is connected to the DUT.

Examples

Example 1: High-speed voltage comparator of the TMU - Direct connection with load

The diagram above shows an example DUT board design setup for time measurements at a low side switch. Both HF# and HS# are connected to the tester side of a load resistor. The TMU pins TMU_H# and TMU_L# are connected to the TMU measurement points via 50 Ω resistors. The 50 Ω termination resistor is mandatory for the TMU_L# pin. The signal at the low sense pin LS# is the reference for measurements using the TMU high-speed voltage comparator.

This recommended setup corresponds to the setup for a (legacy) DC Scale PVI8 card.

Example 2: High-speed voltage comparator of the TMU - Design with multiplexer switches

The diagram above shows how to connect the TMU pins via multiplexer switches to the DUT. TMU_H# is connected to the tester side of the high-side sense switch via a 50 Ω resistor. TMU_L# must be connected to the tester side of the low-side force switch via the shield and a mandatory 50 Ω resistor.
CAUTION Both TMU_L# and LF# must be connected to the tester side of the same multiplexer switch to avoid a high current flowing through the lower 50 Ω resistor and the shield to the internal LF# connection.
Note The voltage drop on the low force multiplexer switch will cause a threshold shift if this setup is used for the (legacy) DC Scale PVI8 card.

Example 3: High-speed voltage comparator of the TMU - Design with multiplexer switches and load

The diagram above shows how to connect the TMU pins via multiplexer switches to the DUT if a load resistor is used. TMU_H# is connected to the tester side of the high-side sense switch via a 50 Ω resistor. TMU_L# must be connected to the tester side of the low-side force switch via the shield and a mandatory 50 Ω resistor.
CAUTION Both TMU_L# and LF# must always be connected to the tester side of the same multiplexer to avoid a high current flowing through the lower 50 Ω resistor and the shield to the internal LF# connection.
Note The voltage drop on the low force multiplexer switch will cause a threshold shift if this setup is used for the (legacy) DC Scale PVI8 card.

Example 4: High-speed voltage comparator of the TMU - Inappropriate connection to LS#

The following diagram shows an inappropriate connection to LS# that results in uncompensated voltage drops and wrong voltage and TMU measurements. This configuration may occur if a DUT board for the (legacy) DC Scale PVI8 card is reused for the FVI16.
Unlike shown in Example 3: High-speed voltage comparator of the TMU - Design with multiplexer switches and load above, TMU_L# is connected to the tester side of the low-side sense switch via the shield and another 50 Ω resistor.
The very low input bias current of the main driver and the TMU low sense input ensures normally that no significant current is flowing on the low / return sense line. For the design as shown above, the expected voltage drop on the force line and force switch will cause a current flowing through the sense switch, the lower 50 Ω series resistor and the shield to the internal LF# connection. This current causes a significant voltage drop at the sense switch.
CAUTION The setup causes a voltage drop on the low-side sense switch that cannot be compensated and falsifies voltage measurements and TMU measurements that refer to the related DUT pin.
Note This setup is acceptable for the high impedance TMU_L# input of a (legacy) DC Scale PVI8 card but not for the FVI16.
