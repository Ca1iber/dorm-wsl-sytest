Avoiding rounding problems with the fract() function

Subtopics

  1. Syntax of fract()
  2. Usage of the fract() function

One method to avoid rounding problems is to define the period with the fract() function. This option is applicable to both Pin Scale and Smart Scale setups.

The basic idea of the fract() function is to keep the least common multiple of the different ECLK periods as small as possible by relating the ECLK periods in an equation that uses the original fractions instead of the resulting decimals. The fractions can then be resolved by multiplying the equation with their denominators. This will lead to a manageable least common multiple if the fractions consist of small integers (or integers that can be made small by cancelling).

If the period is defined with fract(), the shift that distorts the ratio produced by rounding is avoided, or at least minimized, by calculating the master clock period (which is internally represented as a frequency) directly from the fract() definition with expanded precision as 10-9 Hz (nHz). Then the actual user period, which is calculated internally from the MCLK period and an integer multiplier, will reproduce the exact ratio, or at least approximate it sufficiently for most applications. Note that so great a precision is normally not feasible. It is only used in this special case.

fract() can prevent rounding problems for the ECLKs of Smart Scale ports. fract() calculates the least common multiple of their ECLK periods directly from the original definitions. This can avoid a clock overflow, or it can help to determine if the pre-calculated SCLK period is directly divisible by the ECLK periods of a user defined clock group (see Avoiding rounding problems with different clock groups).
