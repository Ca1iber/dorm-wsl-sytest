XPS256 measure voltages

Each channel of an XPS256 card features two combined force, clamp, and measure voltage ranges.

Voltage measurement ranges

Each PMU features two combined force, clamp, and measure voltage ranges:

Variant Range Minimum and maximum voltage
XPS256 1.5 V1) -0.5 V ... 1.5 V
7.0 V -2.5 V ... 7.0 V

The voltage ranges are common for forcing, clamping, and measuring voltages. The force or clamp range is always the same as the measurement range. For specification data, refer to XPS256 voltage specifications. For the maximum current related to the voltage, refer to XPS256 voltage and current capability specifications.

Voltage sense lines

The voltage at the DUT is measured differentially between the high sense pin (PS#) and the ground sense pin (GS#). Both pins are available per XPS256 channel. The high sense pin (PS#) must be connected to the related high force pogo pin(P#) directly at the DUT pins if the Kelvin connection scheme is used. The ground sense pin (GS#) must be connected to the common ground pin (GND) or an adequate DUT pin directly at the DUT both for all connection schemes.

The voltage difference between the force pins and sense pins is typically caused by the voltage drop, produced by current flow over parasitic resistances on the DUT board. A maximum voltage difference must be maintained between the high force pin (P#) and the high sense pin (PS#) and between the ground sense pin (GS#) and the common ground. Violating this rule will result in a wrong force voltage and wrong voltage measurements.

For the maximum voltage difference between P# and PS# and between the common ground and GS# refer to section Force to sense clamp in XPS256 force and clamp voltages. You must also consider the total voltage drop on the force and return line as shown for the Continuous voltage and current capabilities per channel in the XPS256 voltage and current capability specifications.

For parameters and maximum values of the sense pins, refer to table Disconnect state of the XPS256 other performance characteristics.

Measurement filters

XPS256 channels feature programmable anti-aliasing, low-pass measurement filters for all ADCs:

  • Voltage measurement ADC of the PMU
  • Current measurement ADC of the PMU
  • Voltage drop measurements on the force line
  • Voltage drop measurements on the return line
Measurement filter characteristics
Minimum cutoff frequency: 10 Hz
Maximum cutoff frequency: 500 kHz
Default cutoff frequency: 30 kHz

The dynamic behavior of the force output is independent of the measurement filters.

Averaging

All channels of the card feature hardware based averaging of measurements. The average of multiple consecutive ADC samples is calculated and stored on board. Hardware based averaging can be utilized for code‑based and pattern‑based measurements.

Hardware-based averaging
Programmable range of samples: 1 ... 262144 (any value supported)
ADC sample frequency: 2 MHz2)

Some hints to consider:

  • The sample time for averaged measurements must be taken into account.
  • Pattern‑based measurements require a minimum number of vectors after the trigger has been initiated. For more information, refer to Timing of DC events and the related sub-topics.
  • The effective maximum sample frequency is the ADC sample frequency of 2 MHz2) divided by the number of averaged samples.
  • The "measurement filter" may be used instead of averaging if a high effective sample frequency is required. This filter behaves like a simple RC filter. The settling time is to be considered but the filter setting has no effect on the effective maximum sample frequency.
  • The dynamic behavior of the force output is independent of the settings for averaging.

High-impedance voltage measurement mode (HIMP)

A XPS256 PMU features a high-impedance voltage measurement modes. This mode are used to measure the voltage at the DUT with no voltage or current applied. The force output of the PMU is switched to a high-impedance mode. The voltage is measured differentially between the high sense pogo pin (PS#) and the ground sense pogo pin (GS#).

Note The common ground (GND) pins must still be connected in the high-impedance voltage measurement mode for correct common mode operation.

The PMU output is disabled and set to high-impedance for the HIMP modes Previously applied PMU settings have no effect on the input bias current or the dynamic behavior of the disabled PMU output or the sense input.
