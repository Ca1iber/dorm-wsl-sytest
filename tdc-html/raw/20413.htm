Topic 20413

DUT board stiffener manufacturing guidelines

Before designing DUT boards and their stiffeners, obtain all the information about the mechanical features of the handler docking plate and the contactor to determine if additional holes or keep-out areas are required.

Do not mill the stiffener to provide additional clearance for surface trace lines or components on the DUT board. Such milling decreases the mechanical stability of the assembly. Attempt to route surface trace lines in areas where the stiffener and inlay do not contact the board and place components in the available mounting areas. Whenever possible, place signal lines on inner layers of the DUT board. If milling is absolutely necessary, mill as little as possible to maintain mechanical stability. Avoid milling in the mounting screw areas, as this can cause damage to the board when it is mounted to the stiffener. Refer to drawings D-E6980-96530, D-E6980-96540, and D-E6980-96550 in Layout information: DUT board for standard DUT board keep-out areas for quarter-size, half-size, and full-size DUT boards, respectively.

Advantest is not responsible for direct or indirect damage caused by the use of non-Advantest stiffeners.

Use clear anodizing on all aluminum stiffener surfaces and use hard-chromium electroplating on all steel surfaces. Stainless steel surfaces must be passivated to minimize oxidation on the stainless steel. Advantest recommends a coating thickness of 10 to 15 µm. All dimensions shown for the stiffeners are final dimensions after coating. Anodizing is not a reliable method and is used mainly for cosmetic reasons. Advantest uses clear anodizing; other types are also acceptable.

All custom third-party stiffeners or inlay plates must have the company name, logo and part number engraved on the top. Two engraved arrows on the top and on the bottom surface of the stiffener must be included that point toward the manipulator when the stiffener is properly installed on the DUT interface.

Drawings E6980-64110 (512 pin) and E6980-64120 (1024 pin) show the mechanical dimensions, tolerances, and machining instructions for each stiffener (see Stiffener common parts).
