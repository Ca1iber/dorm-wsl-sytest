# Shielding and guarding

## Shielding

Shielding usually implies using a metal enclosure to prevent electrostatic interference from affecting a high impedance circuit.

To minimize the effects of electrostatic generated currents, keep all charged objects (including body parts like hands) away from the test circuit area. Minimize any sudden movements of objects near the DUT or DUT board area. Also minimize any vibration of equipment.

## Guarding

Guarding usually means the use of a low impedance conductor that has the same potential as the high impedance input lead of the measurement circuit.

A guard greatly reduces any interfering voltage or current and also pre-charges any dielectric between the guard and the high impedance conductor.

The charged dielectric helps to reduce measurement times as the dielectric is already at the applied measurement potential.

However, a guard does not necessarily provide shielding of a conductor. Also, a guard potential is not ground. A guard potential is always at the signal's potential. Therefore never ground the guard connection.

The guard trace is positioned between the signal trace and all other traces. The guard trace supplies the leakage current between the signal trace and the other traces. The guard trace keeps the potential around the signal trace as close to zero as practical. When the voltage difference between traces is close to zero, the leakage current is also close to zero.

## Guard routing

For high precision measurements of small currents, route the guard trace around force and sense lines to minimize current leakage.

The guard DPS channel is not connected to the device and it is charged to the same voltage as the pins powering the device.

Note On some DC Scale power supply cards there is no dedicated guard pin. In this case you can use an adjacent force pin and route the guard traces around the sensitive force and sense traces. In the example shown below the force pin is indicated as P (power) and sense is PS (power sense).
