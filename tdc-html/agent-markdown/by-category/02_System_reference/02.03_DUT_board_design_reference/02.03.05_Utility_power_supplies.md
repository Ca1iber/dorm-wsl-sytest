Utility power supplies are used to power active components like relays, operational amplifiers, and buffer on the DUT board or probe card. In contrast to device power supplies, the utility power supplies provide fixed voltages and do not provide a programmable clamp current or measurement functionalities.

## Common characteristics of all utility power supplies

Utility power supplies can work in only one quadrant: They can force a positive voltage and drive a current, or they can force a negative voltage and sink a current. The output can be switched on or off.

 As a result, the utility power supplies cannot force 0 V at the output and cannot discharge circuitry on the DUT board. The following notes must therefore be observed:

- The time to bring the voltage down to 0 V depends on the load and the capacitors on the DUT board. High load currents will decrease the time. Large capacitors will increase the time.
- Switching off a utility power supply for only a short time can lead to a critical voltage drop. See also the description for [UP5V on Pin Scale and SmartScale systems](<02.03.05_Utility_power_supplies.md#_346875__r_UP5V>) in [Utility power supplies](<02.03.05_Utility_power_supplies.md>).
- The critical drop in the supply voltage, also known as brownout, can bring digital circuits into an undefined state.
- The current through the internal pull‑up resistor of a deactivated utility lines and the connected circuitry on the DUT board can also push up the output voltage of a switched‑off utility power supply to an undefined value. For more information, refer to [Utility line characteristics](<02.03.04.02_Utility_line_characteristics.md>) and [Utility line example circuits](<02.03.04.03.03_Utility_line_example_circuits.md>).
