# General rules to follow

## Avoid humidity and dirt

Leakage can also be caused by humidity. Excessive humidity on a test floor can reduce or seriously affect the measurement results of high impedance, low current paths. Try to minimize relative humidity (RH) in test floors to less than 50%.

Dirt, fingerprints (oils) and moisture on the surface of a PCB or on a component are the main sources of leakage. Therefore route these traces in inner layers and buried vias whenever possible.

High contact resistance will cause a voltage drop in the circuit path. The connector pins should always be clean. Use a high quality, corrosion free material for all pads and contact points.

## Keep boards clean

And most importantly, keep the DUT board and connector surfaces as clean as possible. Thoroughly clean them after soldering and do not touch them. Always wear gloves or finger protection when handling low leakage boards. To minimize contamination touch boards only by the edges.

CAUTION Body fluids are conductive and corrosive.

When cleaning any board or circuitry on it, use only pure solvents to remove contamination or dissolve any surface oils and solder flux. After you have cleaned the area with a solvent, rinse it again with new, pure methanol or deionized water. Allow the cleaned area to dry for several hours in a humidity controlled environment.

## Temperature

Test temperatures can affect low current measurements in several ways. Temperature swings can cause thermal expansions at pogo pins and connectors. This will produce noise currents on high impedance measurement paths. To minimize this problem, the entire test system should be maintained at a constant temperature. Because the V93000 system is water cooled for temperature stability, this problem has been minimized.

## RF sources

High frequency RF energy sources can affect low current measurements. This effect can be observed as a sudden change in a low current measurement for no obvious reason. Some possible and potential RF sources can be electronic solenoid valves on handles and process gas lines, cellular phones, high voltage transformers, high voltage switches and local RF transmitters. To solve this problem you must first identify the source and then eliminate it with shielding and filter networks.

## Minimize number and size of pads

Also the number and size of pads on the surface of a DUT board should be minimized.

Keep the distance between the traces as wide as possible to reduce the parasitic capacitances. Also avoid parallel routing of signal traces and power traces to minimize the capacitive noise pick up. Large current flows or quickly changing currents in power traces can generate transient fields. These power traces should not be in close proximity to signal traces.
