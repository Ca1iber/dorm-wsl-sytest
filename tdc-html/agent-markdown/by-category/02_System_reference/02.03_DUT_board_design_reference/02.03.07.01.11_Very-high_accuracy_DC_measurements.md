# Very-high accuracy DC measurements

Specific rules and DUT board best practices apply when making highly accurate DC measurements. In general, it is not a specific section in the board routing that degrades the accuracy: Usually many small contributors add up into the overall accuracy. Therefore, as a board designer, you need to keep all these creeping additional small voltage steps at low levels - to achieve the effects of added skew at a required minimum level.

## Low path resistance

The main goal is to achieve low path resistance in all critical paths. Keep IR drop at a minimum. This is especially true if you have no local voltage sensing at the DUT. Digital cards (like the PS1600) typically do not feature sense lines per pin.

Mind your routing both in the Force/Sense and Ground/Ground Sense side.

## Use of relays

Be mindful of using relays: Keep the thermo EMF contribution at minimum. Thermo electric voltages are generated by relays because they contain different metallic materials. At different temperatures, these materials behave as thermocouples and generate a voltage depending on the temperature difference.

In practice, each relay can add 10 to 20 uV thermal EMF, or less. Some relays may have a negative thermo EMF. This can help minimizing the overall relay EMF effect.

## Transitions

In general, each transition has a small but definite potential difference that adds into the overall voltage measurement accuracy. Over the course of several such junctions, many small voltage drops add up and create a measurable voltage skew. Therefore, the general rule is:

- Minimize the number of transitions.
- If a transition is necessary, minimize its size and thus its effect.

## Dissimilar materials

Dissimilar materials are like microscopic batteries: they add to the skew of voltage. Therefore, avoid connecting dissimilar materials. Typically, vias alone are not necessarily a problem.

For example, going from one layer (such as a copper pour) to another layer (similar copper pour) through a copper via will not cause any measurable voltage drop. However, a routing path from the copper via into a gold plated pad in the top or bottom layer does create a dissimilar material issue.

Similarly, going from a gold plated pad into a connecting pogo (likely of beryllium copper) causes an additional voltage difference - which can have an effect as added noise in the overall measurement accuracy. These voltage steps add up and create residual uncertainties, meaning, even after-calibration uncertainties.

## Ferrite beads and inductors

Avoid ferrite beads and inductors in the signal path. Ferrite beads, especially older types, may have internal dissimilar material issues by construction. Inductors, on the other hand are vulnerable to have radiated effect: magnetic coupling. While these devices may be needed and are justified in power supply (DPS) channels, there is no reason to use them in signal channels or sense lines.
