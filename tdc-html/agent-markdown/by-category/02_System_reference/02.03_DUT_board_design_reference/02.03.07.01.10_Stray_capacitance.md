# Stray capacitance

Stray capacitance is unwanted or undesired capacitance.

Stray capacitance is a function of the conductor area, the distance between adjacent conductors, and the dielectric constant of the material supporting the conductors.

- The greater the distance or separation of the conductors the lower the stray capacitance. The closer the distance between conductors, the higher the stray capacitance.
- The larger the area of the conductors, the higher the stray capacitance.

At a constant thickness, wide traces can carry more current than narrow traces. This changes the cross-sectional area of the conductor and has a large effect on any stray capacitance.

At a constant width, thick traces can carry more current than thin traces. This changes the cross-sectional area of the conductor and has a minimal effect on any stray capacitance.
