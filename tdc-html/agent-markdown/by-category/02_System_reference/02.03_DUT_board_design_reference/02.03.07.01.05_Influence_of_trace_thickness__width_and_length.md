# Influence of trace thickness, width and length

The trace thickness is measured vertically to the laminate structure, while the trace width and length is measured horizontally.

To avoid unnecessary capacitance and leakage, design all copper traces for very low currents only as thick as necessary.

 Note Increasing the trace thickness reduces the trace's resistivity and has no effect on stray capacitance.

The trace width, however, has a direct affect on the stray capacitance and any leakage currents, because it is an integral part of the trace surface area. Also the distance between traces has a direct effect on the stray capacitance and leakage currents.

At a constant thickness, wide traces can carry more current than narrow traces. This changes the cross-sectional area of the conductor and has a large effect on any stray capacitance.

At a constant width, thick traces can carry more current than thin traces. This also changes the cross-sectional area of the conductor but has only a minimal effect on any stray capacitance.

Wide traces can also couple AC-noise from other sources. This is because their large surface areas can easily pick up leakage currents.
