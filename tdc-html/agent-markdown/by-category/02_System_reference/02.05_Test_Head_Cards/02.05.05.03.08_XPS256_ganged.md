# XPS256 ganged

In order to achieve a higher output current, parallel connection of multiple XPS256 channels is supported. This is called "ganging".

## Ganging

Ganged XPS256 channels can be setup under user control or under software control:

- User鈥慶ontrolled ganging:

Several channels are simply connected in parallel and similar settings are applied. This can lead to an undefined distribution of the current to the individual channels and may result in unstable output voltages and invalid current measurements. Modifying the settings may improve the behavior but needs more attention of the user.

- Software鈥慶ontrolled ganging:

The parallel connected channels are defined as ganging group in the pin definitions of SmarTest. The ganging group is accessed as single pin. The on鈥慴oard-ganging controller distributes the total output current equally between the ganged channels. This solution will show a stable output signal and provides reliable current measurements without applying special setups as required for ganging under user control.

We recommend therefore, always setting up software鈥慶ontrolled ganging.

## Ganging characteristics

An XPS256 channel can be ganged together with any other XPS256 channel in any order under the following conditions:

- Ganging is supported for any number of channels within a card.
- Flexible ganging allows channels to be assigned to a ganging group, even if they are not consecutive.
- Flexible ganging across XPS256 cards is supported for up to 4096 channels.
- The number of ganging groups is

  - limited to 128 for ganging within an XPS256 card,[1)](<#fntarg_1>)
  - system-wide limited to 32 for ganging across XPS256 cards.

- For XPS256, ganging is supported in the 1 A range of the PMU exclusively, even if less current is required or available. The maximum current per channel is limited as follows:

  - 1.0 A for voltages up to 3.0 V
  - 0.6 A for voltages up to 7.0 V

For information regarding the maximum current of ganged XPS256 channels, refer to Maximum continuous voltage and current capability per card in [XPS256 voltage and current capability specifications](<../02.02_Specifications/02.02.05.03.04_XPS256_voltage_and_current_capability_specifications.md>).

## On-board ganging controller

The on-board ganging controller is used to balance the total output current between the ganged channels.

In a ganged configuration, the channel with the lowest number acts as the master channel[2)](<#fntarg_2>). The master channel feeds the measured current to the slaves and each slave uses this value to adjust its own current to be identical with the master's current.

## DUT board connections

Some connections of the force lines and sense lines of ganged channels are required on the DUT board:

- The P# (high force) pins of the ganged channels must be connected together on the DUT board.
- The same parallel connection is required for the PS# (high sense) pins and the GS# (ground sense) pins of the ganged channels. The combined force lines and sense lines must be connected together at the DUT.
 CAUTION The pins (P#) and (PS#) are part of the digital closed loop control of the same channel. P# and PS# must therefore always be connected to the same DUT pin. Violating this rule may result in an unpredictable behavior and cause a current of some mA flowing via the internal force to sense clamp between (P#) and (PS#).

Since the XPS256 channels are digitally regulated VI supplies and the on-board ganging controller uses internal data paths to balance the output current between the ganged channels, no external control connections are required between ganged XSP256 channels. This applies also for ganging across XPS256 cards.

## Checking the gang connections

SmarTest provides the functionality to check the connection of ganged channels. Since the return line is common for all channels of a card, the check is performed only for the force (P#) lines. It is expected that the individual ground sense (or return sense) lines (GS#) have already been checked using the [XPS256 Kelvin test unit](<02.05.05.03.03.04_XPS256_Kelvin_test_unit.md>).

 The measurement is carried out as follows:

- The sense to force connection is verified for all individual channels of the ganging group.
- The master channel of the ganging group forces 0.0 V at the ganging connection on the DUT or on the DUT board. Both the force line and the sense line of the master are connected for precise setting of the voltage.
- All slave channels simultaneously force a low test current via the force line P# to the ganging connection on the DUT or on the DUT board.
- The slave channels measure the voltage drop on the P# line, the resulting resistance per line is calculated, compared with a fixed limit of 20 O and returned as go/no鈥慻o result for all channels.

For information on how to setup a check of the gang connections, refer to [Checking the gang connections](<02.05.05.03.10.10_XPS256_programming_-_Ganging.md#_354657__s_Checking_the_gang_connections>) in [XPS256 programming - Ganging](<02.05.05.03.10.10_XPS256_programming_-_Ganging.md>).

## Application notes

  Resolution of the force or clamp current All channels of a ganged group are programmed to the same values. The resolution of the programmed force or clamp current is the resolution of a single channel multiplied by the number of ganged channels. High-resolution current comparators are silently disabled in temporarily unganged mode

Note that on DPS128/64 and XPS256 you cannot execute a current compare on a ganged group of channels in unganged mode. Already activated comparators are silently disabled in unganged mode. A user notification (Error or warning) is omitted in this case, to avoid any interrupt in the testflow. The previous state of the comparators is automatically restored after the ganged group has been restored.
