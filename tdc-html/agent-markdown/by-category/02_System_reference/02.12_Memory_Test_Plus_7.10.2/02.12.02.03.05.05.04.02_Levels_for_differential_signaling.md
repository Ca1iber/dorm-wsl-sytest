# Levels for differential signaling

#### Subtopics

- [Complementary pins](<02.12.02.03.05.05.04.02.01_Complementary_pins.md>)
- [Pin loading for differential signaling](<02.12.02.03.05.05.04.02.02_Pin_loading_for_differential_signaling.md>)
- [Differential Context](<02.12.02.03.05.05.04.02.03_Differential_Context.md>)

For most applications, [single-ended signaling](<02.12.02.03.05.05.04.01_Levels_for_single-ended_signaling.md>) is adequate, but for certain applications (such as high-speed memory testing) the limitations of single-ended signaling become a significant issue. Two problems in particular are of concern:

- The voltage swing of a single-ended signal tends to be smaller in high-speed applications (this makes it easier to accommodate high speeds), but making the voltage swing too small can cause the signal to be lost in the noise, so that a 1 cannot be reliably distinguished from a 0.
- In single-ended signalling the voltage level is referenced to ground. In some systems the ground may "float" upward, making the threshold point indeterminate.

For these reasons, many high-speed applications use differential signaling, in which each signal is on a pair of [Complementary pins](<02.12.02.03.05.05.04.02.01_Complementary_pins.md>).

Differential signaling, although it more reliably distinguishes 1s and 0s at high speed, has the disadvantage that, for every differential signal, two pins are required on the device, and two channels are required on the tester.
