# Analyzing bit fail maps

#### Subtopics

- [Available information](<02.12.04.04.02.04.01_Available_information.md>)

- [Changing the color scheme](<02.12.04.04.02.04.06_Changing_the_color_scheme.md>)

Once you have loaded the bit fail maps, you can analyze them in a variety of ways. Options include working on the entire bit fail map or just a part, examining large-scale patterns and details, marking and commenting cells, highlight cells meeting specific conditions, and choosing colors for failure combinations.

## About this task

The exact steps you take for analyzing a bit fail map depend on the purpose of your analysis, the contents of the fail maps, and not least on your personal preferences.

This task describes options that you should apply as needed in your work.

## Before you begin

The bit fail maps must have been loaded, as described in [Loading bit fail maps into the BFM Viewer](<02.12.04.04.02.03_Loading_bit_fail_maps_into_the_BFM_Viewer.md>).

## Procedure

 The exact steps for analyzing a set of bit fail maps is different for each case and depends on the questions you want to answer and the data you have.

Common actions include:

- [Reviewing the available information](<02.12.04.04.02.04.01_Available_information.md>)
- [Displaying details](<02.12.04.04.02.04.02_Creating_and_managing_detail_views.md>)
- [Restricting the BFM Viewer's reports](<02.12.04.04.02.04.03_Specifying_an_analysis_area.md>) to a part of the DUT
- [Applying filters](<02.12.04.04.02.04.05_Using_filters.md>) to highlight cells meeting certain conditions
- [Annotating and marking](<02.12.04.04.02.04.04_Marking_and_unmarking_cells.md>) the bit fail maps
- [Adapting the colors](<02.12.04.04.02.04.06_Changing_the_color_scheme.md>) to make important information easier to detect
- [Exporting information](<02.12.04.04.02.06_Exporting_information.md>) to discuss it with colleagues or process it with other tools

Apply these procedures as needed.
